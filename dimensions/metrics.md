# Metrics

What to measure about human-agent teams and their delivery, and what controlled studies and delivery metrics show.

## Where the experts agree
- Measure delivered outcomes, not activity. Lines generated, commits, PR counts, acceptance rates and token counts can rise with no real gain, and agents now produce tests and docs so cheaply that those artefacts no longer signal quality. ([Thoughtworks: Measure internal quality and rework, not generation speed](../principles/thoughtworks/measure-internal-quality-and-rework-not-generation-speed.md), [Gergely Orosz: Measure delivery outcomes, not AI activity](../principles/orosz/measure-delivery-outcomes-not-ai-activity.md), [Addy Osmani: Measure outcomes and comprehension, not activity](../principles/osmani/measure-outcomes-and-comprehension-not-activity.md), [Simon Willison: Judge output by real use and quality, not artefacts](../principles/willison/judge-output-by-real-use-and-quality-not-artefacts.md), [Anthropic engineering: Measure verified outcomes with noise-aware evals](../principles/anthropic/measure-verified-outcomes-with-noise-aware-evals.md), [METR: Fix the outcome measure before the work starts](../principles/metr/fix-the-outcome-measure-before-the-work-starts.md))
- Always read throughput next to stability and quality, against a pre-AI baseline. A team that ships more while change failures, rework or regressions rise is not improving. ([DORA: Measure delivery by both throughput and instability](../principles/dora/measure-delivery-by-both-throughput-and-instability.md), [DORA: Speed and stability are not a tradeoff](../principles/dora/speed-and-stability-are-not-a-tradeoff.md), [Thoughtworks: Measure internal quality and rework, not generation speed](../principles/thoughtworks/measure-internal-quality-and-rework-not-generation-speed.md), [Charity Majors: AI amplifies discipline, so measure outcomes alongside throughput](../principles/majors/ai-amplifies-discipline-so-measure-outcomes-alongside-throughput.md), [Gergely Orosz: Measure delivery outcomes, not AI activity](../principles/orosz/measure-delivery-outcomes-not-ai-activity.md))
- Do not trust impressions. Judge models, harnesses and agent changes with repeated runs on the team's own tasks, and only act on gains larger than measurement noise. ([Birgitta Böckeler: Measure the harness and agent setup with data](../principles/bockeler/measure-the-harness-and-agent-setup-with-data.md), [Anthropic engineering: Measure verified outcomes with noise-aware evals](../principles/anthropic/measure-verified-outcomes-with-noise-aware-evals.md), [METR: Measure AI speedup instead of trusting perception](../principles/metr/measure-ai-speedup-instead-of-trusting-perception.md))
- Do not turn metrics into per-person targets or AI-usage quotas. Targets invite gaming, so use several metrics that pull against each other for the team's own improvement. ([DORA: Use metrics for a team's own improvement, not as targets or rankings](../principles/dora/use-metrics-for-a-team-s-own-improvement-not-as-targets-or-rankings.md), [Charity Majors: No individual AI-usage quotas](../principles/majors/no-individual-ai-usage-quotas.md), [Gergely Orosz: Measure delivery outcomes, not AI activity](../principles/orosz/measure-delivery-outcomes-not-ai-activity.md))

## Where they disagree
- **Is token spend a useful signal?**
  - Yes, while teams ramp up. Spend shows how much people practise with agents, up to roughly 15M tokens a day per engineer, after which you switch to outcomes and waste per token. ([Steve Yegge: Measure token spend on the way up, then waste and outcomes](../principles/yegge/measure-token-spend-on-the-way-up-then-waste-and-outcomes.md))
  - No. Token counts reward looking busy, and reviewing people on tokens pushes them to burn tokens. ([Gergely Orosz: Measure delivery outcomes, not AI activity](../principles/orosz/measure-delivery-outcomes-not-ai-activity.md), [Thoughtworks: Measure internal quality and rework, not generation speed](../principles/thoughtworks/measure-internal-quality-and-rework-not-generation-speed.md))
  - Evidence to settle it: Whether rising token spend per engineer during ramp-up predicts later gains in lead time and stability, or only predicts more spend.
  - Metric: Daily tokens per engineer plotted against lead time, change failure rate and rework for the same engineers a quarter later.
- **Are DORA delivery metrics enough to judge an agent-heavy team?**
  - Yes, the five DORA metrics tracked per service, with throughput paired with instability, describe delivery health. ([DORA: Measure delivery by both throughput and instability](../principles/dora/measure-delivery-by-both-throughput-and-instability.md), [DORA: Measure each application in its own context](../principles/dora/measure-each-application-in-its-own-context.md))
  - No. DORA can look healthy while fewer people understand the codebase and agent output is rubber-stamped, so add review minutes, cost per merged PR and a comprehension measure. ([Addy Osmani: Measure outcomes and comprehension, not activity](../principles/osmani/measure-outcomes-and-comprehension-not-activity.md))
  - Evidence to settle it: Cases where DORA metrics stay flat or improve while comprehension falls, followed later by worse incidents or slower recovery.
  - Metric: A periodic comprehension check (for example, engineers explaining a recent agent-written change) tracked next to time to restore and change failure rate.

## Measured
- In METR's randomized trial on real backlog tasks in mature open-source repos, experienced developers took 19% longer with AI allowed, yet expected a 24% speedup beforehand and still believed in a 20% speedup afterwards. The slowdown was linked to high quality bars and unwritten conventions the model could not see. ([METR: Measure AI speedup instead of trusting perception](../principles/metr/measure-ai-speedup-instead-of-trusting-perception.md), [METR: Tacit knowledge and implicit standards limit AI in mature codebases](../principles/metr/tacit-knowledge-and-implicit-standards-limit-ai-in-mature-codebases.md))
- DORA's survey data found AI adoption associated with rising delivery instability, and found that extra throughput did not offset instability's harm to product performance and burnout. Speed and stability were correlated, not traded off, across teams. ([DORA: Speed and stability are not a tradeoff](../principles/dora/speed-and-stability-are-not-a-tradeoff.md), [DORA: Measure delivery by both throughput and instability](../principles/dora/measure-delivery-by-both-throughput-and-instability.md))
- DORA found reliance on AI for core work more meaningful than usage frequency, and process gains alone did not yield better delivery. Majors reports that large AI gains go to teams that already work in short feedback loops and measure outcomes. ([DORA: Judge AI by downstream impact, not adoption](../principles/dora/judge-ai-by-downstream-impact-not-adoption.md), [Charity Majors: AI amplifies discipline, so measure outcomes alongside throughput](../principles/majors/ai-amplifies-discipline-so-measure-outcomes-alongside-throughput.md))

## Open hypotheses
- An append-only per-agent track record (completions, reopens, cost, independently validated quality) lets a team route work and widen or narrow agent autonomy in a way that lowers rework. ([Steve Yegge: Attributable agent identities with an honest, permanent track record](../principles/yegge/attributable-agent-identities-with-an-honest-permanent-track-record.md))
  - How to test it: Attribute every agent change to an agent identity and owner, then compare reopen and rework rates when work is routed on that history versus assigned without it.
