# Number of people

How many people a team needs and how team structure changes when agents take on work.

## Where the experts agree
- Agents let a small team deliver work that used to need a much larger one, so projects are being staffed with fewer people. Thoughtworks has reported this across several projects, and Orosz and Yegge from practice. ([Thoughtworks: Agents shrink and reshape teams, but not down to one person](../principles/thoughtworks/agents-shrink-and-reshape-teams-but-not-down-to-one-person.md), [Gergely Orosz: Fewer engineers per project, but keep senior oversight](../principles/orosz/fewer-engineers-per-project-but-keep-senior-oversight.md), [Steve Yegge: Small, oversubscribed human teams](../principles/yegge/small-oversubscribed-human-teams.md), [Charity Majors: Smaller teams with blurring roles](../principles/majors/smaller-teams-with-blurring-roles.md), [Addy Osmani: Favour small cross-functional teams of end-to-end engineers](../principles/osmani/favour-small-cross-functional-teams-of-end-to-end-engineers.md))
- As teams shrink, roles blur. Staff small cross-functional teams with people who have breadth, such as end-to-end engineers and analysts in flexible roles, rather than separate specialist groups. ([Thoughtworks: Agents shrink and reshape teams, but not down to one person](../principles/thoughtworks/agents-shrink-and-reshape-teams-but-not-down-to-one-person.md), [Steve Yegge: Small, oversubscribed human teams](../principles/yegge/small-oversubscribed-human-teams.md), [Charity Majors: Smaller teams with blurring roles](../principles/majors/smaller-teams-with-blurring-roles.md), [Addy Osmani: Favour small cross-functional teams of end-to-end engineers](../principles/osmani/favour-small-cross-functional-teams-of-end-to-end-engineers.md), [Kent Beck: Use agents to break down discipline silos](../principles/beck/use-agents-to-break-down-discipline-silos.md))
- There is a limit to shrinking. When too many engineers each run agents they get in each other's way, so a small team still needs clear module boundaries and a deliberate way to keep a shared understanding of the system. ([Gergely Orosz: Fewer engineers per project, but keep senior oversight](../principles/orosz/fewer-engineers-per-project-but-keep-senior-oversight.md), [OpenAI engineering: A small team with many agents needs large-organisation structure](../principles/openai/a-small-team-with-many-agents-needs-large-organisation-structure.md), [Thoughtworks: Agents shrink and reshape teams, but not down to one person](../principles/thoughtworks/agents-shrink-and-reshape-teams-but-not-down-to-one-person.md))

## Where they disagree
- **Can one person directing agents replace a whole team?**
  - Possibly. Martin offers it only as a conjecture: one person could do the work of five or six developers, so teams become individuals who each own a large component. ([Robert C. Martin: One person can do a team's work behind narrow interfaces](../principles/unclebob/one-person-can-do-a-team-s-work-behind-narrow-interfaces.md))
  - No. A stable team of a few people, from about two or three up to about seven, is still needed to hold the shared mental model and to provide senior oversight of agent output. ([Thoughtworks: Agents shrink and reshape teams, but not down to one person](../principles/thoughtworks/agents-shrink-and-reshape-teams-but-not-down-to-one-person.md), [Gergely Orosz: Fewer engineers per project, but keep senior oversight](../principles/orosz/fewer-engineers-per-project-but-keep-senior-oversight.md), [Steve Yegge: Small, oversubscribed human teams](../principles/yegge/small-oversubscribed-human-teams.md), [OpenAI engineering: A small team with many agents needs large-organisation structure](../principles/openai/a-small-team-with-many-agents-needs-large-organisation-structure.md))
  - Evidence to settle it: Run comparable services side by side, some owned by one person with agents and some by a team of two to five with agents, for several months. Compare what each delivers, how reliable it is, and how well it copes when the owner is away.
  - Metric: Incident rate, time to detect problems and change lead time per service, by number of human owners
- **As agent throughput per person rises, should human coordination go down or up?**
  - Down. Each person owns a large component and coordinates with others only through narrow interfaces, so there is less collaboration and more output. ([Robert C. Martin: One person can do a team's work behind narrow interfaces](../principles/unclebob/one-person-can-do-a-team-s-work-behind-narrow-interfaces.md))
  - Up. Strict boundaries are needed, but so is more synchronous sharing, such as a long daily standup, because no single person can follow the state of the code. ([OpenAI engineering: A small team with many agents needs large-organisation structure](../principles/openai/a-small-team-with-many-agents-needs-large-organisation-structure.md), [Thoughtworks: Agents shrink and reshape teams, but not down to one person](../principles/thoughtworks/agents-shrink-and-reshape-teams-but-not-down-to-one-person.md))
  - Evidence to settle it: Track coordination time and integration failures as agent use per engineer grows, and check whether teams that cut meetings in favour of interface contracts see more integration breakages.
  - Metric: Merge collisions and cross-service integration failures per week, against hours of synchronous coordination per engineer

## Open hypotheses
- Small teams do better when they have more worthwhile work than people. Spending agent gains on growth, rather than on cutting headcount, keeps teams in that state. ([Steve Yegge: Small, oversubscribed human teams](../principles/yegge/small-oversubscribed-human-teams.md), [Kent Beck: Use agents to grow, not just to cut headcount](../principles/beck/use-agents-to-grow-not-just-to-cut-headcount.md))
  - How to test it: For each team, track the backlog of approved, valuable work per person alongside throughput and team conflict. Compare teams that kept headcount flat and took on new initiatives with teams that were cut.
- Cutting senior infrastructure and security staff when shrinking a team increases incidents, because those people are the ones who check agent output. ([Gergely Orosz: Fewer engineers per project, but keep senior oversight](../principles/orosz/fewer-engineers-per-project-but-keep-senior-oversight.md))
  - How to test it: Before and after any reorganisation, track senior headcount on critical services against incident frequency, severity and time to detect.
- Letting product people and other non-engineers ship their own changes through agents shortens the time from a decision to production without hurting quality. ([Kent Beck: Use agents to break down discipline silos](../principles/beck/use-agents-to-break-down-discipline-silos.md))
  - How to test it: Measure the share of changes shipped by or paired with non-engineers, the lead time from decision to production, and the change failure rate, before and after merging disciplines into one team.
