# Expect AI to help most where you know least

Verified: 3 verbatim quotes from 1 source

Dimension: Roles for agents (agent-roles)

Evidence: measurement

How to measure: Break down AI-allowed and AI-disallowed completion times by how much prior exposure the developer rates themselves as having to the task, and by codebase size or maturity.

AI help is worth the most on unfamiliar tasks, codebases or APIs and on small greenfield projects, and the least where the developer is already an expert. Lean on it in unfamiliar territory, be sceptical of it in code you know deeply, and don't generalise one setting's result to others.

## Quotes

> developers are slowed down more when they have high prior task exposure and lower external resource needs.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity at p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089#page=19)

> For example, our results are consistent with small greenfield projects or development in unfamiliar codebases seeing substantial speedup from AI assistance.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity at p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089#page=12)

> In our study, developers often note that AIs lack this tacit codebase knowledge, resulting in less useful AI outputs.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity at p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089#page=20)
