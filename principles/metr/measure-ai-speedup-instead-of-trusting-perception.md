# Measure AI speedup instead of trusting perception

Verified: 4 verbatim quotes from 2 sources

Dimension: Metrics (metrics)

Evidence: measurement

How to measure: Randomly assign real backlog tasks to AI-allowed and AI-disallowed conditions, then compare measured completion times with the developers' forecasts and after-the-fact estimates of their speedup.

Developers and experts overestimate how much AI speeds up their work, sometimes getting even the direction wrong, and the overestimate persists after hours of use. Judge productivity by controlled measurement of real work, not by self-reports, surveys or forecasts.

## Quotes

> This gap between perception and reality is striking: developers expected AI to speed them up by 24%, and even after experiencing the slowdown, they still believed AI had sped them up by 20%.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)

> And we now have strong evidence that anecdotal reports/estimates of speed-up can be very inaccurate.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)

> Furthermore, we show that both experts and developers drastically overestimate the usefulness of AI on developer productivity, even after they have spent many hours using the tools.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity at p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089#page=10)

> the importance of conducting field experiments with robust outcome measures, compared to relying solely on expert forecasts or developer surveys.
>
> [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity at p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089#page=12)
