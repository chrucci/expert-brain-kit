# Human cognitive capacity limits agents per person and team size

Verified: 7 verbatim quotes from 3 sources

Dimension: Number of agents (agent-count)

Evidence: experience

How to measure: Track concurrent agent sessions and code owned per engineer against fatigue, defect rates and incident response time, to find where quality drops.

Code production is no longer the bottleneck; human attention and comprehension are. One engineer can sustainably supervise only about three or four parallel agents for a few hours, and teams still need several engineers to share the cognitive load of a growing codebase, because a team of one is badly designed.

> I can fire up four agents in parallel and have them work on four different problems. And by like 11 AM, I am wiped out for the day.
>
> [Highlights from my conversation about agentic engineering on Lenny's Podcast](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/2/lennys-podcast/)

> There’s a personal skill we have to learn in finding our new limits—what’s a responsible way for us not to burn out.

> I often have three projects that I’m working on at once because then if something takes 10 minutes I can switch to another one and after two hours of that I’m done for the day.
>
> [My fireside chat about agentic engineering at the Pragmatic Summit](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/14/pragmatic-summit/)

> the answer is that the new limiting factor is cognitive capacity. I can churn out code a hundred times faster. I don’t have the cognitive capacity to stay on top of 100 times the amount of code.

> So you still need a team of engineers, so you can load balance that cognitive capacity across the team.

> a team of one is a very badly designed team
>
